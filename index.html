02 / First entries

Four notes to begin the conversation.

Entry 001 · Attention
What we return to becomes easier to notice; what we notice becomes easier to understand.

Attention behaves like a quiet form of capital. Repeated contact builds context, and context lets subtle patterns become visible. The practical question is not only what deserves focus today, but what deserves repeated focus over years.

Read beside: Meditations

Try: Choose one subject you want to understand in five years. Give it one undistracted hour each week.

03 / Book notes

A starter shelf, distilled one book at a time.

Book 01

Meditations

Marcus Aurelius · philosophy

Distilled argument

We do not control events, only the quality of attention and action we bring to them.

What to keep

Character is built in ordinary repetitions. Philosophy matters most when it changes the next response.

Connected note: 001 · Attention compounds
Book 02

The Psychology of Money

Morgan Housel · markets & behavior

Distilled argument

Financial outcomes depend as much on behavior, time, and survival as on technical intelligence.

What to keep

Room for error is not wasted capacity. It is what lets a sound plan survive an uncertain world.

Connected note: 002 · Judge the process
Book 03

The Beginning of Infinity

David Deutsch · knowledge & progress

Distilled argument

Problems are soluble when we can create better explanations and keep criticism open.

What to keep

Optimism is not a forecast that things will go well; it is a stance that improvement remains possible through knowledge.

Connected note: 003 · Tools shape questions
Book 04

Tao Te Ching

Laozi · philosophy

Distilled argument

Force often defeats itself; restraint, adaptability, and non-grasping can create a more durable kind of power.

What to keep

Enough is not a number discovered at the end of striving. It is a boundary chosen before striving becomes endless.

Return ritual

Once a week, revisit one old note before adding a new one. Mark what still feels true, what now feels incomplete, and which other idea it should sit beside. The book grows by connection, not accumulation.
